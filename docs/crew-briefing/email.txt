Subject: How we'll send you crew requests from now on

Hi everyone,

A quick heads-up about a change on our end that affects how you'll hear from us about work.

From now on, every crew request from Luminary goes out through our new scheduling system instead of a scattered mix of texts, calls, and one-off emails. You get a proper call sheet for every job; you can take the job or pass on it in a couple of taps; and once you're booked, the details stay in your pocket.

There's nothing for you to install. No app to download, no account to create, no password to remember. It all works from a link in your email — on your phone or your computer.


1. THE REQUEST ARRIVES BY EMAIL

You'll get an email from us with a subject line like "Crew request: Autumn Summit 2026 — Luminary Technology & Productions."

It lists the project, the location, and every call we're asking you to cover — date, call time, wrap time, and your role. If a production manager has left a note on a shift (parking, gate code, wardrobe, catering), that's in there too.

There's one button: View & Respond.

[SCREENSHOT 1 — the request email]


2. TAP "VIEW & RESPOND" TO OPEN YOUR CALL SHEET

That button opens a web page we call your call sheet. It shows only the calls we're asking you to cover on this job: the project, the venue with a map to the address, and each shift in order with its times, your role, and any notes.

Read your call sheet through before you answer. Everything we know about the job is on that page, and it stays there for you to come back to.


3. ANSWER WITH ONE OF TWO OPTIONS

Two buttons sit at the bottom of your call sheet:

  • Accept These Calls — you're taking every shift listed on your call sheet.
  • I Can't Make It — you're passing on the job.

Whichever button you choose, you can add a short note before you submit. Please use it — that note comes straight back to us.

Accepting is also the first step in getting paid. Your acceptance is what starts the payment process for that job, so a shift you never accepted can hold up your pay. Please answer every request you get, even one we've already talked through.

If you can only do some of the days: each request covers all of its calls as a single block, so there is no way to accept some and decline others on the same page. Choose "I Can't Make It", then use the note to tell us which days do work — something like "Can't do the Saturday load-in, but Friday and Sunday are fine." We'll read it and usually send you a fresh request covering just those days. Replying to the email works too.

[SCREENSHOT 2 — your call sheet, with the two buttons and the note]


4. ACCEPTING IS NOT THE SAME AS BEING BOOKED

This is the part people most often get wrong.

Tapping "Accept These Calls" puts you down as penciled in, and your call sheet will say "awaiting confirmation." A production manager still has to confirm you before the job is yours.

When that happens you'll get a separate confirmation email, and your call sheet will change to "You're confirmed." That email is what makes the job yours, so please don't count on the work until it arrives. Your call sheet always shows where you currently stand.

While you're penciled in, put the dates in your own calendar and hold them. If something else comes up for those dates and you still haven't heard back from us, call or email and let us know — we would much rather re-plan early than lose you on the day.


5. ADD YOUR CONFIRMED CALLS TO YOUR CALENDAR

Once a production manager has confirmed you, your call sheet shows an "Add to Calendar" button beside each call. One tap drops that call's date, times, role, location, and any notes straight into your own calendar — so you still have the details when there's no signal at the loading dock.

[SCREENSHOT 3 — the confirmed call sheet with Add to Calendar]


A FEW THINGS WORTH KNOWING

  • Your link is yours. The link in your request email opens your call sheet without asking for a password, so please don't forward it — anyone who has it could answer in your place.

  • Your answer locks once you submit it. You can't change it yourself, so if your availability changes, reply to your request email or call us and we'll sort it out on our end.

  • We'll email you about changes too. If a call time moves, a production manager adds a note to one of your shifts, or a booking has to be cancelled, you'll get an email spelling out exactly which shifts are affected.

  • Agreed a job by phone or text? You'll still get a request email for it, and you still need to accept it. We always send the request, however we first agreed the work — and shifts arranged by phone are the ones most likely to go unaccepted, which is the most common cause of delayed pay.

  • Keep your contact details current. Requests go to whichever email address we have on file for you, so please tell us if yours changes.
